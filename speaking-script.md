# Speaking script — English is a Thief

## 1 — Title

Hello everyone. Our topic today is: English is a thief.

We want to show you where English words really come from.

## 2 — 170,000 words

English has about 170,000 words that people use today.

But here is our question: how many of them are actually English?

Take a guess. Half? More?

## 3 — Only 1 word in 4

*(the names above the bar are hidden — press the arrow key to reveal them one
by one while the class guesses)*

Before I show you: which languages do you think English stole from?

*(let them shout, then reveal)*

This first block is the original English — only 25 percent. One word in four.

Three out of four English words were taken from another language.

But one thing is important. The words we use every day — *the*, *is*, *man*,
*water* — those are still the old English ones.

English borrowed the big vocabulary. Not the basics.

## 4 — Where English comes from

So before we talk about the stealing: where does English itself come from?

It starts north of the Black Sea, about 6,500 years ago. In what is now
Ukraine and southern Russia.

That language is called Proto-Indo-European. Nobody ever wrote it down. We
only know it by comparing the languages that came out of it.

From there it spread across Europe — and all the way to India. That is why
English and Hindi are distant cousins.

And only from Old English on do we call it English.

This family line is exactly the 25 percent from the slide before. Everything
else came from outside.

## 5 — One sentence, 1000 years

This is the same sentence in four periods of English.

Watch the word for *father*. *Fæder. Fadir. Father. Father.*

Old English is basically a foreign language. You cannot read it.

Same sentence, same language — one thousand years apart.

## 6 — The timeline

So this is who English stole from.

The black box on the left is the original English. Everything else came later.

Let me show you the most important ones.

## 7 — Why did English borrow so much?

Every one of these had a reason.

Latin came with religion and education. Old Norse with invasion. French with
conquest. Greek with science. And the rest came with trade.

Whenever English speakers met new people, English picked up their words.

So let's look at how that happened — starting with Latin.

## 8 — Latin

Latin came twice.

First around the year 600, with the Christian missionaries. They taught and
wrote in Latin. That is where *school*, *street* and *wine* come from.

Then again around 1500, when science exploded. Nobody invented words like
*data* or *formula*. They just took Latin a second time.

Latin is 28 percent of English. Almost a third of the dictionary. We just
stopped noticing.

## 9 — The Vikings

The Vikings attacked England for 200 years. Then they stopped attacking, and
just lived there.

The two languages mixed, and English kept their words.

*Window* is a Viking word — *vindauga*. It means wind-eye. A hole for the wind.

And English also took *they*, *them* and *their*.

These are basic words. Most languages never borrow words like this.
English did.

## 10 — The French

1066. William the Conqueror wins.

For the next 300 years the kings of England speak French. But the farmers keep
speaking English.

So the farmer in the field says *cow*. The rich man at the table says *beef*.

Same animal. Two words.

The poor kept the animals, the rich ate them. And a thousand years later we
still use both words.

## 11 — Out into the world

From 1600, British ships went everywhere.

They came home with tea and cotton — and with words.

*Shampoo* is not an English word. It is Hindi, and it means to press, or to
massage.

This is how English became a world language. Not because it is easy. Because
of ships and power.

## 12 — Quiz

Now a quiz. Six words. Where do you think they come from?

Ketchup is Chinese — it used to be a fish sauce.
Chocolate is from Mexico.
Sofa is Arabic. Robot is Czech. Tsunami is Japanese. And zero is Arabic too.

## 13 — Korean words

And English is still stealing. Right now. From us.

In September 2021, the Oxford English Dictionary added 26 Korean words in
one day.

*Hallyu. Mukbang. Oppa. Daebak. Skinship.*

A thousand years ago it was the Vikings. Today it is Korea.

## 14 — Konglish

And it works in the other direction too. Korea makes its own English.

Some of these an American could actually guess. *Hand phone* is probably a
mobile phone.

*Fighting!* is different. They know the word — they just would not know why
you are shouting it at them.

And most Americans have never heard *skinship* at all.

But everyone in this room knows all six.

So — is this wrong English? Or is it just Korean English?

## 15 — Ending

English is a thief.

And that is exactly why it belongs to everybody.

Thank you.
