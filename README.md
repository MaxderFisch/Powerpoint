## What to say

**1 — Title**
"Our topic is: English is a thief. We want to show you where English words really come from."

**2 — 170,000 words**
"English has about 170,000 words that people use today. But how many of them are really English? Take a guess."
→ let the class guess, then click.

**3 — Only 1 word in 4**
"Only 25 percent. Three out of four English words were taken from another language."
"But careful — the words we use every day, like *the*, *is*, *man*, *water*, those are still the old English ones. English borrowed the big vocabulary, not the basics."

**4 — The timeline**
"This is who English stole from. The black box on the left is the original English. Everything else came later."
→ Don't read all of it. Point at 2–3 boxes. Then: "Let me show you three of these."

**5 — The Vikings**
"The Vikings attacked England for 200 years. Then they stopped attacking and just lived there. The two languages mixed."
"*Window* is a Viking word. It means wind-eye — a hole for the wind."
"And then this." → show *they / them / their*
"These are basic words. Most languages never borrow words like this. English did."

**6 — The French**
"1066. William the Conqueror wins. For 300 years the kings of England speak French, but the farmers keep speaking English."
"So the farmer in the field says *cow*. The rich man at the table says *beef*. Same animal, two words."
"The poor kept the animals, the rich ate them. And 1,000 years later we still use both words."

**7 — Out into the world**
"From 1600, British ships went everywhere. They came home with tea and cotton — and with words."
"*Shampoo* is not an English word. It's Hindi, and it means to press or to massage."
"This is how English became a world language. Not because it is easy. Because of ships and power."

**8 — Quiz**
"Six words. Where do you think they come from?"
→ Let the class shout, then click. Don't do all six if we are short on time — ketchup and robot are the best ones.

**9 — Korean words**
"And English is still stealing. Right now. From us."
"In September 2021 the Oxford English Dictionary added 26 Korean words in one day."
"A thousand years ago it was the Vikings. Today it is Korea."

**10 — Konglish**
"And it works in the other direction too. Korea makes its own English."
"An American would not understand *hand phone* or *eye shopping*. 50 million Koreans do."
"So is this wrong English? Or is it just Korean English?" → ask the class, wait.

**11 — Ending**
"English is a thief. And that is exactly why it belongs to everybody. Thank you."
